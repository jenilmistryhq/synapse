Sudden Death - Ashcombe Observatory

Incident26-01-0117
Reported07:14 WED 07 JAN
OfficerPS G. ORMEROD
LocationASHCOMBE OBSERVATORY
Sub-locationMAIN DOME
DeceasedHALE, Prof. E. (62)

Circumstances

At 07:14 the caretaker, Thomas BRACKLEY, telephoned to report that the observatory's director, Professor Edmund HALE, was lying dead on the floor of the main telescope dome.

Mr Brackley states that he found the dome door bolted from the inside at about 07:05. He fetched a crowbar from the plant room and forced the door at 07:10. The bolt keeper was torn from the frame. He found Professor Hale at the foot of the observing platform ladder.

The dome has one door. The only other opening is the observing slit in the roof, which was open to the sky and is six metres above the floor. The room was locked from the inside, with the deceased alone in it.

Scene

The deceased lay on his back on the steel floor plate beside the base of the platform ladder. The platform is 2.4 m high and is reached by a vertical steel ladder. The dome is unheated; the scene thermometer read -3 C. The dome lights were off; his head torch was on, battery nearly flat. His laptop was open on the platform desk, screen asleep. His spectacles were found under the platform.

Officer's Summary

Professor Hale was working alone in a locked dome at night, in the cold and the dark, on a ladder. Injuries are consistent with a fall from the platform (see B-1). No third party could have entered or left the dome. Provisional finding: accidental death. Referred to the coroner. No further police action.

Reopened 02 MAR. The deceased's sister has written to the coroner stating that her brother "had climbed that ladder ten thousand times." Referred to the Case Review Unit with the coroner's consent.

Post-Mortem Examination - Hale, Edmund

CaseOCME 26-0031
DecedentHALE, E. 62 M
Examined08 JAN 10:20

Injuries

1. Occipital (back of the head). Linear fracture of the occipital bone with an overlying laceration, 40 mm. Consistent with the back of the head striking a hard, straight edge, such as the edge of the steel floor plate.

2. Vertex (crown of the head). A circular depressed fracture, 34 mm across, with a fine criss-cross patterned abrasion on the scalp over it. Attributed to the head striking a ladder fitting during the fall.

Either injury could have caused death. Bruising around both injuries shows that both were received while he was alive.

Other findings

No defensive injuries. No injury to the hands or wrists. Blood alcohol nil. Toxicology negative. Mild coronary artery disease, not contributory.

Time of death

The body lay in an unheated dome at about -3 C with the roof slit open. Cooling in these conditions is too fast and too uneven for a useful estimate. Death occurred between about 23:00 on 06 January and 03:00 on 07 January. Any narrower time must come from other evidence.

Conclusion

Cause of death: head injury. Manner: provisionally accidental, on the police account that he was alone in a room locked from inside.

Note. A person falling from 2.4 m would usually strike the head once. Two head injuries on opposite surfaces of the skull, one of them patterned, are unusual in a fall. I have accepted the police account of the scene.
