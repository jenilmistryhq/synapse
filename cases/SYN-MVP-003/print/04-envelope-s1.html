The Ashcombe Dome

Step 1 · Not a fall

A fall strikes the head once. Edmund Hale had two head injuries, on opposite sides of his skull.

The one on the crown is a 34 mm circle with a criss-cross pattern. Nothing on the ladder or the rail has that surface (E-1.3). The brass counterweight on the platform rack does: a 34 mm knurled grip. It was the only clean one of the four, and his blood and hair were still in the knurling.

Evidence: B-1 (two injuries, one patterned), E-1.2 and E-1.3, and SLIP 03 or SLIP 09.

Step 2 · When

He was alive at 01:37:12, when he stopped the telescope with the hand paddle in the dome. Nobody touched the telescope again; it tracked on until it hit the horizon at 03:52.

The killer left the dome at 01:52:18. Edmund Hale died between 01:37 and 01:52.

Evidence: D-2 (hand paddle 01:31 - 01:37, then nothing), SLIP 01 (exit 01:52).

Step 3 · There was no locked room

The door was never forced from outside. The splinters are on the inside of the frame, and the pry marks are on the inside edge of the door.

At 07:08:12 Thomas Brackley's code opened the dome door, and it swung open: a bolted door cannot move. He went in, closed the door, threw the hand bolt, levered the keeper off the frame from inside, and walked out through a door that now looked forced. Then he rang 999.

Evidence: E-1.1 (damage on the inside), SLIP 01 (#4476 OPEN, DOOR MOVED), against C-1.

Step 4 · The forged log

D-1 is a filtered printout made by the man who forced the door.

It jumps from event #4471 to #4478, and its own footer says the period held 11 events while it shows five. The six it leaves out are exactly the six that matter: Lindqvist in at 01:24, Lindqvist out at 01:52, and Brackley's own code at 07:08. With them gone, the log said what the first officer believed: nobody went in after Hale.

Evidence: D-1 (sequence gap, count mismatch, "FILTERED", printed by T.BRACKLEY), SLIP 01 (the full record).

Step 5 · The killer

Ivo Lindqvist said he never went near the dome.

His code opened the dome door at 01:24:33. His workstation had no input from 01:23:40 until he unlocked it at 02:06:30, so "I watched every frame come in" is false: the sequence had finished at 01:20. He left the dome at 01:52:18. At 02:11 he searched for how to find blood on cleaned brass, and at 02:19 for how to delete a door event.

Evidence: SLIP 01 (#4472, #4474), SLIP 02, D-2 (sequence complete 01:20), against C-2.

Step 6 · The cover-up

Thomas Brackley did not kill anyone. He was not in the building until 07:02 (D-3). He made the murder into an accident.

Lindqvist rang him at 03:18, thirteen minutes after leaving. At 06:41 $4,000 arrived in his account from Lindqvist, marked "boiler". Twenty-seven minutes later he staged the locked door, and that morning he printed the police a door log with the night taken out of it. He told the police nobody rang him.

Evidence: E-1.1, SLIP 01, D-1, SLIP 04, against C-1.

Step 7 · The cleared

Dr Miriam Okafor was in Geneva; no card of hers was used (D-3, SLIP 06). Ellen Hale never entered the building and her car never left her drive (D-3, SLIP 07). Kasia Nowak left at 23:41 and was on the last bus (D-3, SLIP 08). Nothing in the building opens from outside without a card, and Hale was alive at 01:31.

Step 8 · Why

Edmund Hale had found out that Lindqvist's comet was not his. The discovery images had been assembled from another survey's data, and the observatory's own mount log proved the telescope had been pointed elsewhere that night. At 01:16 Hale saved a letter to the journal. He meant to put it to Lindqvist "tonight" and send it "in the morning whatever he says".

Evidence: SLIP 05, with C-3 ("a problem in somebody's data"), C-4 and C-5 ("Tonight, Ivo").

Murder

IVO LINDQVIST

with the cover-up by THOMAS BRACKLEY

What happened

The imaging finished at 01:20 and Lindqvist went up. Hale was at the paddle, nudging the telescope east to show him, from the mount log, where it had really been pointing. The argument lasted a few minutes. Lindqvist picked a counterweight off the rack and struck him once from above. Hale fell backwards off the platform edge onto the floor plate.

Lindqvist wiped the weight, put it back, and walked out at 01:52, leaving the door shut but unbolted. He sat in the control room for an hour, searching for how to undo what he had done, then drove away and rang the one man who could change the door log.

Brackley's price was $4,000. He bolted a dead man in, broke the door open from the wrong side, and printed a log that ended at 21:58. The first officer saw a locked room and a ladder, and did the rest himself.

The lock told the truth. It just told it to two places, and only one of them could be edited.
