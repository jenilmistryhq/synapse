Terms of Reference · Ravensgate

Case Review — Sarn and Ballard (Deceased)

On the night of 18 November, Storm Bettany closed the road to Ravensgate Dam and six people were sealed in with each other until the following afternoon. By dawn two of them were dead.

Aurelio Sarn, the Station Manager, was electrocuted in the turbine hall at 06:03. Ines Ballard, a Control Engineer, was found drowned at 07:25 in the intake gallery. Ms Ballard's credential card had opened the cabinet Mr Sarn died at, and had closed the circuit that killed him.

The police concluded murder–suicide and closed the file in nineteen days.

The Reservoirs & Dam Safety Inspectorate does not agree, on a technical point about water, and has said so in writing. This review is convened because two competent authorities have reached findings that cannot both be true.

Your task

You are the review team. Between you, you hold every file both enquiries generated — but no one of you holds all of it.

Establish what happened at Ravensgate. If the original finding is wrong, say so, say who, and show your working.

What is different about this case

The file is split. The Case Board in the middle of the table is public and belongs to everybody. Everything else is in five Specialist Files, and each of you holds one. You may not read another detective's file. You may not pass it across. You may only table what you choose to table, when you choose to.

This is not a game of hidden traitors. Nobody at this table is lying to anybody. Every one of you wants the same answer. The difficulty is that the answer is in five pieces and the pieces do not know about each other.

A warning about a dead woman

This review begins with a conclusion already reached about Ines Ballard, who is not here to argue with it. Nineteen days of police work say she killed a man and then drowned herself. If you decide they were right, say so — that is a legitimate finding and you may write it down.

Just be sure it is a finding and not an inheritance.

Solo play does not use the Conference. See the reverse of the Resolution Sheet.

2 · Three Sessions

The review proceeds in three Sessions. Take a breath between them; they are not timed.

In each Session, each detective may TABLE one document from a file they hold. To table it: read it aloud, then lay it face up on the Case Board. It is public from that moment and stays public.

One document. Per detective. Per Session. Choose it well — and notice that what is worth tabling in Session 3 is not what was worth tabling in Session 1.

3 · The Query

At any moment, any detective may QUERY: name a subject, a piece of plant, a person or a clock time.

Every other detective must answer truthfully whether their file holds anything on it. Yes or no. They need not say what it is, and tabling it still costs a Session slot.

Queries are free and unlimited. Asking the right question is the skill this case is testing.

4 · Authorities

Between Sessions — never during one — the table may spend from a shared pool of Authorities, exactly as in Case 01. Twelve lines of inquiry are listed overleaf.

5 · After Session 3

All files open. Everything is shared, everyone reads everything, and the review reaches its finding with the whole file in front of it.

But: each step of the Case Resolution Sheet scores double if it was established on the Case Board before the end of Session 3. Reaching the right answer after everything is face up is worth something. Reaching it while the file was still in pieces is worth twice as much.

6 · Accusing

Two wrong accusations are permitted; each costs 10 points and returns a Reconsider envelope. Your third accusation is final. Then, and only then, open Envelope S-1.

Four of these twelve return nothing you do not already have. They are not traps; they are the ordinary shape of an investigation, and the skill is telling them apart from the others before you pay.
