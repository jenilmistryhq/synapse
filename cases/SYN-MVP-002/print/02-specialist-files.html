Note on credentials at a console

A logged-on operator session and a credentialled control action are separate records. The session says who is signed in. A control action on a permissioned circuit additionally requires a card to be presented at the console reader at the moment of the action. The two need not be the same person, and the system does not require them to be.

Operations note: this record shows only that the session was open. It does not, and cannot, show whether the operator was in the chair.

Statement

  1. The gallery is sealed. There are three openings: intake gate 2, the upstream inspection hatch, and the draft tube. There are no others.
  2. The inspection hatch was dogged shut from the storm preparation on 18 November at 16:40 until it was opened by the recovery team at 07:25 on 19 November. It is bolted from outside. It cannot be opened from within the gallery.
  3. The draft tube ran under vacuum continuously while unit 1 was generating, which it was all night without interruption. Flow through the draft tube is outward and downward at all times. Nothing enters the gallery this way. Nothing can.
  4. The upstream stoplogs were in place and sealed for the duration of the storm, as logged in the storm-preparation checklist.
  5. Therefore the only route from the forebay to trash rack panel 4 is through intake gate 2, while intake gate 2 is open and flowing.
  6. Whenever intake gate 2 is shut, the gallery is dry above the sill at 211.0 m. The body was at 218.4 m — seven metres above the sill. There is no standing water at that elevation with the gate shut. There is nothing there at all.

Conclusion of this statement. The deceased passed through intake gate 2 while it was open and flowing. Cairnhold Civil offers no view on how she came to be in the forebay. We say only that she was carried into the gallery by water, and that the water was only there for as long as the gate was.

How the monitor works — and why this is not a signature on a sheet

A personal atmospheric monitor is issued against the permit and worn on the entrant. It transmits every fifteen seconds to the control room for as long as it is below the wellhead — oxygen, hydrogen sulphide, methane, and a received-signal figure that falls away sharply with depth. The telemetry is what tells the surface that somebody is still down there and still breathing.

It cannot be left at the bottom of the well and walked away from. The signal profile of a stationary monitor and a worn one are not alike, and the unit alarms on thirty seconds of no motion.

Lockout–tagout, in plain terms

Before working on switchgear, you isolate the circuit at the breaker, apply your own padlock to the isolator, hang your tag on it, and then prove the circuit dead with a tester you have first proved on a live source. Nobody can remove your lock. Nobody can re-energise past it. It is a good procedure and it has kept people alive for eighty years.

It protects the circuit you locked. It says nothing whatever about a second circuit you did not know existed.

Finding of This Report

Mr Sarn did everything correctly.

He isolated feeder F-5. He applied his personal padlock and his signed tag. He proved the circuit dead with a tester that this department has since examined and found accurate. The cabinet was dead when he opened it and he had proved it so.

He was then killed at 06:03 by feeder F-3, a second supply to the same busbar, closed remotely from the control room at 06:02:14.

Question this department cannot answer. The as-built schedule for C-14 held in the control room — the drawing an engineer consults before isolating — shows this cabinet as single-fed from F-5. It does not show F-3. Whether that is an error in the drawing, or something else, is outside our competence and outside this report. Cairnhold engineering records hold the amendment history.

The array was never installed and has been reporting for five years. The values submitted to this Inspectorate quarterly since 2019 were generated by something other than an instrument in the ground.

Ms Ballard's observation on the night — that the reported values did not respond to reservoir level — is correct, and is the signature of synthetic data. A real foundation piezometer under a rising reservoir cannot fail to move.

Consequence

The actual foundation pore pressures at Ravensgate are unknown and have been unknown for five years. The Inspectorate has issued a Section 10 direction. The station is operating under restriction pending a full drilling and instrumentation programme.

This is a matter of very considerable regulatory gravity independent of the two deaths, and the Inspectorate wishes it recorded that it is not a small thing that happened alongside a murder. It is the reason there was one.
