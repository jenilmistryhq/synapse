# Project Synapse case writing

Write like a person who has a reason to put these words on the page. Case documents belong to a named institution, a particular writer, and a particular moment. Interface copy should sound like a calm colleague at the table.

## Before drafting

- Read the case bible, ledger, adjacent documents, and relevant case-writing notes first. Treat them as canon. Never invent a fact that changes the solution.
- Identify the speaker, audience, purpose, date, and pressure behind each document. An evidence log, a private email, and an interview transcript should not share one voice.
- Keep the chronology and evidence references exact. Separate what a character knows from what the player can infer.

## Make the prose sound lived-in

- Prefer concrete nouns, active verbs, and details that could only belong to this case.
- Let characters disagree, hesitate, correct themselves, omit things, and use different sentence rhythms. Give each voice its own vocabulary and habits.
- Let official documents be clear but not eerily polished. Real records contain routine phrasing, useful specifics, and occasional awkwardness; do not add typos as decoration.
- Cut generic scene-setting, repeated summaries, tidy three-part lists, inflated transitions, and conclusions that restate the evidence.
- Avoid canned suspense, melodrama, faux-poetic clues, and statements that explain the intended deduction to the player.
- Do not use vague filler such as “it is worth noting,” “in this moment,” or “a sense of unease.” Replace it with an observable action or remove it.

## Make dense reading easier

- Give each page one clear job. Break long documents with useful headings, short paragraphs, tables, and labelled sections.
- Keep paragraphs focused on one event or claim. Vary sentence length; do not make every line a fragment.
- Put dates, names, quantities, and record references where a reader can scan for them.
- Explain specialist terms at first use in plain language, preferably through the document's own voice.
- Add interaction only when it helps the player inspect, compare, mark, listen to, or discuss evidence. Do not turn every paragraph into a quiz.
- Preserve the evidence trail: a clue should be discoverable and citeable in the printed and digital versions.

## Edit interface copy

- Say what the control does in a few ordinary words. Use a verb.
- Tell players what happens next when the action is consequential.
- Keep hints specific and non-spoilery. Offer a next investigative action before giving away an inference.
- Never claim that voice recordings, notes, or private group work are private unless the implementation makes that true.

## Check before shipping

- Compare every factual claim against the case bible and ledger.
- Read dialogue aloud. If every character sounds like the same writer, revise it.
- Read the page as a tired player: can they find the names, times, and evidence without rereading a wall of prose?
- Verify exact clue phrases, citations, word counts, print layout, and spoiler boundaries with the project's existing tools.
